## Pitch, verbatim (as reported)
> An AI assistant for the visually impaired that processes visuals from a chest-mounted phone camera and responds via voice or controller, eliminating touchscreen reliance. […] The developer's brother, who is blind, played a vital role in ensuring features were genuinely helpful for the blind community.

## The wow moment
The phone is strapped to the chest, so hands stay free for the cane. The user clicks a tiny game controller and hears what's in front of them, with no screen at all.

## Scope reality
- Real hardware setup (chest mount + 8BitDo Micro controller + voice commands), on-device Gemma 3n.

## Stack
Gemma 3n on-device (Google AI Edge). The on-device part *is* the prize category.

## Why this won (one sentence)
A named real person (the developer's blind brother) shaped every design decision, and the on-device constraint was treated as a feature (privacy, no signal needed), not a limitation.

## Transferable to us
- **Copy:** design for how the user physically lives (hands busy, screen useless). Make "on-device, nothing leaves your machine" a headline feature. Credit real input honestly.
- **Don't copy:** a named user story we don't actually have. **Rishik has no Deaf collaborator, so we cite published DHH research instead of inventing one.**
